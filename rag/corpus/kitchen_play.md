# Kitchen (Non-Volley Zone) Play

## What the Kitchen Is

The kitchen is the non-volley zone (NVZ): the 7-foot box on each side of the net where you
cannot volley (hit the ball out of the air). Its full name reflects its function — it forces
players into a soft game of patience. Mastering kitchen play is the single biggest skill
separator in recreational and competitive pickleball.

## The Dinking Game

A dink is a soft shot hit from at or near the kitchen line that arcs gently over the net and
lands in the opponent's NVZ. The purpose is not to win the point outright but to force the
opponent into a difficult or unattackable return.

**Why dink instead of driving?** When both teams are at the kitchen line, the net is only
7 feet away. A hard drive at close range gives opponents very little reaction time — but it
also means a slight mis-hit goes out or into the net. A controlled dink resets tempo and
forces the opponent to dink back or attempt a low-percentage attack.

**Cross-court dinks** are the highest-percentage dink. The ball travels a longer diagonal
distance (roughly 24 feet vs 16 feet straight ahead), giving it more room to clear the net
and still land in the kitchen. Aim for the deepest part of the cross-court kitchen, pulling
the opponent wide.

**Down-the-line dinks** are more aggressive — less margin for error but can expose the
opponent's backhand or create a difficult angle.

## Dink Mechanics

- Contact the ball at or just below net height. Getting low (bend your knees) is critical.
- Use a continental or eastern grip. The wrist stays firm; the shoulder drives the motion.
- Keep your paddle face open (slightly tilted back) on low balls to pop the ball up and over.
- Follow through toward your target, not up — a high follow-through often floats the ball up
  into the attackable zone.

## Resets from the Kitchen

A reset is a defensive dink hit when you are under pressure — a hard drive coming at you, or
your team out of position. The reset has one job: get the ball to land softly in the opponent's
kitchen with no pace, forcing them back into a dinking exchange.

**Technique for a reset:** Absorb the pace of the oncoming ball by loosening your grip at
contact. Think of "catching" the ball with your paddle rather than blocking it. The paddle
face stays open; the swing is minimal — mostly the ball's own pace carries it over.

**When to reset vs attack:** Reset when the ball is below the net or when you are off-balance.
Attack when the ball is above net height and you have a stable stance. Many beginners attack
balls they should reset, leading to unforced errors.

## Poaching and Switching

When your partner is pulled wide by a cross-court dink, the middle of the court opens. The
other partner should slide toward center to cover, and be ready to poach — take a ball
heading toward their partner if it is more attackable from their position.

**Poach signals:** A small gesture (paddle tap, verbal "mine") prevents both players from
going for the same ball. Communication collapses many kitchen errors.

## Attacking from the Kitchen

Attack opportunities arise when:
- The opponent's dink floats above net height (paddle is above your wrist on contact)
- The opponent pops the ball up — a result of hitting with topspin or too much pace
- You have created an extreme angle by pulling them wide

**Speed-up:** A sudden accelerated drive aimed at the opponent's shoulder (the "chicken wing"
zone) gives minimal reaction time. After a speed-up, expect a counter and be ready with a
tight, compact swing.

**Roll shot:** Brushing up the back of the ball with topspin drops it quickly after clearing
the net. Useful cross-court when the opponent is leaning the wrong direction.

## Common Mistakes

- **Lifting the elbow:** Causes the paddle to dip under the ball, leading to pop-ups.
- **Rushing:** Impatience at the kitchen is the most common cause of unforced errors. Take
  the extra dink to create a better attack opportunity.
- **Standing too far from the kitchen:** If you are 2-3 feet behind the line you lose the
  angle advantage. Crowd the line as much as possible.
- **Attacking into the net:** When you do attack, aim for the lower half of the net's height
  above the crossing point. Many attackers aim too high.
