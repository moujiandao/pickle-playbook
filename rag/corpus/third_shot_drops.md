# Third Shot Drops

## Why the Third Shot Matters

In pickleball, points most often start like this:
1. Server hits a deep serve
2. Returner hits a deep return — and immediately rushes the net
3. Server's team must decide: drive or drop?

The serving team starts at the baseline. The returning team, if disciplined, is already at the
kitchen line by shot 3. The serving team is therefore at a massive disadvantage: they are 20+
feet from the net against two opponents who can volley aggressively.

The **third shot drop** solves this. Rather than driving the ball hard (giving opponents an
easy volley), you hit a soft, arcing shot that lands in the opponent's kitchen. Because they
cannot volley in the NVZ, they must let it bounce — and that gives you time to advance.

## The Goal of the Third Shot Drop

The third shot drop is not a winner. It is a transition shot. Its only job:

1. Force the opponents to let the ball bounce (buying you time to run forward)
2. Land in the kitchen so they cannot attack it hard when they do return it
3. Give you and your partner time to reach the kitchen line

If the drop lands softly in the kitchen and they return with a dink, you should already be at
the transition zone (around the service line) or kitchen line. Success.

## Mechanics

**Stance:** Open or semi-open stance. Weight on the front foot at contact.

**Swing path:** Low to high. Contact the ball below your waist. The upward swing imparts
slight underspin or flat contact, which helps the ball die in the kitchen rather than carry
long.

**Paddle face:** Open (slightly tilted back). Combined with the low-to-high swing, this
lifts the ball with control.

**Arc:** The ball should travel in a high, slow arc — sometimes called a "rainbow" trajectory.
Aim to clear the net by 1-2 feet. A flat, low trajectory is too risky: the net will catch it.

**Target:** The middle of the kitchen (between the two opponents) is the safest target.
Aiming wide is riskier and gives opponents an angle.

## Common Errors

**Hitting it too hard:** The most common mistake. A drop hit with too much pace bounces up
into the attackable zone. Opponents will crush it. Slow down. Commit to the soft shot.

**Hitting it too short:** A drop that bounces near the kitchen line instead of deep in the
kitchen is attackable. Aim for the back third of the kitchen.

**Not advancing after the drop:** The third shot drop means nothing if you stay at the
baseline. As soon as you hit it, move forward. Watch the opponent's return and be ready to
reset if needed.

**Looking up too early:** Eyes on the ball through contact. Many players look up to see where
it's going and mishit the shot.

## Transition Zone Play

After a good third shot drop, you may not make it all the way to the kitchen before the
opponent's return arrives. This is the **transition zone** — roughly from your service line to
about 5 feet behind the kitchen.

In the transition zone:
- Take the ball out of the air (volley) if it is above the net — do not let it drop to your
  feet, which forces a very difficult low shot.
- If the ball is at your feet, hit another drop. Reset. Refuse to be pushed back.
- Split-step as the opponent contacts the ball so you are balanced and ready to move.

## Fourth Shot Drop (When the First Fails)

If the third shot drop pops up and opponents smash it back at your feet, you may need a
fourth shot drop — essentially the same shot, same purpose. It's rare to get all the way to
the kitchen in one transition. Good players often need 2-3 drops to work their way up.

**Patience wins the transition.** Rushing leads to attacking with a ball that is too low,
creating errors and putting you on defense. Each successive drop gets you a little further
up the court.

## Third Shot Drive (Alternative)

Not every third shot must be a drop. A **third shot drive** is a hard, flat shot hit with
pace and purpose. Use it when:
- The return bounces high (above your waist) — the ball can be attacked effectively
- You want to disrupt the opponent's rhythm at the kitchen line
- Your partner is already close to the net and can capitalize on a defensive return

After a third shot drive, do not expect to advance. You are trading the transition advantage
for a chance to win the point directly or force a pop-up.

The decision rule: if the return is below your waist, drop. If it is above your waist,
consider driving.
